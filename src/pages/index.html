What people carry into this office

Most clients arrive with the same worries. Here is what each one sounds like, and what we do about it.

  1. I got a letter and I do not know what it means, but the date on it is close.

    What we doBring the letter, every page, the day you call. We read it, tell you in plain words what it is asking, and put the deadline on the firm's calendar before anything else happens.

  2. Someone told me they could get my papers fixed for cash. No lawyer, no contract.

    What we doOnly a licensed attorney or a Department of Justice accredited representative can represent you before immigration authorities. Anyone else, however friendly, is risking your record. You can verify any Texas lawyer's license on the State Bar of Texas website, including ours.

  3. I am afraid one wrong answer on a form will cost me years.

    What we doEvery filing is prepared and reviewed by the attorney, not handed to a form service. Your answers are checked against your history before anything is signed, because consistency is what the government reads for.

  4. I do not know if my situation even qualifies for anything.

    What we doThe first consultation is an honest assessment. If there is a path, we tell you what it is, how long it tends to take and what it costs. If there is no path right now, we tell you that too, and what would have to change.

  5. English is not my first language and I am worried I will miss something.

    What we doBring a family member or an interpreter. We slow down, we write the next steps out, and nobody leaves this office unsure of what happens next.

  6. My family is split between here and home, and every option seems to risk one side.

    What we doFamily petitions, consular processing, waivers and adjustment of status are weighed together, not one at a time. The plan is built around keeping your family whole, within what the law allows.

  7. I cannot sit in an office for hours. I have work and children.

    What we doCall ahead and we set a time. Come prepared with the items on the prepare your call list and the first meeting stays short and useful.
