Before you pay anyone for immigration help

Immigration fraud takes money from people who can least afford to lose it, and it can leave a permanent mark on your record. A few rules keep you safe.

  • In Texas, a notary public is not a lawyer. A "notario" cannot give legal advice or represent you before immigration authorities.
  • Only a licensed attorney or a Department of Justice accredited representative may represent you. Ask for the bar number and check it on the State Bar of Texas website.
  • Nobody can guarantee a result, promise a special relationship with USCIS, or sell you a place in line. Walk away from anyone who does.
  • Never sign a blank form. Never let anyone file a form in your name without showing you the completed copy first.
  • Get a written agreement that says what the fee covers, and get receipts for every payment.

Why the paperwork matters this much

The government reads your file for consistency across years: dates, addresses, names, prior applications. A small contradiction becomes a question at interview, and a question can become a denial.

So your history is gathered first and every form is checked against it before it is signed. Slow at the start, faster everywhere after.
